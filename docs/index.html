Accessibility Analysis

Statistical insights into Swiss public transport accessibility patterns and inequalities

Question: Do better connections guarantee higher prices?

Real estate prices vary enormously across Swiss municipalities (CHF 800-31,000/m²), yet travel times show surprisingly modest correlation. Some expensive areas have long travel times to IC hubs; some remote areas offer affordability with excellent access.

Question: Are job centers well connected to IC hubs?

Striking finding: All 25 Swiss cantons show remarkably uniform travel times to IC hubs (173-177 minutes average). Employment distribution appears independent of rail accessibility—suggesting Swiss rail infrastructure provides equitable connectivity nationwide regardless of canton size or economic weight.

Question: Does prosperity correlate with better transport access?

Income varies across Swiss cantons (CHF 70,800-78,600 per capita), but IC accessibility remains nearly constant (~175 minutes). Regional wealth does not predict rail connectivity. Geographic location, not economic prosperity, determines IC accessibility across Switzerland's 25 cantons.

Question: Does density guarantee or prevent isolation?

Population density does not uniformly predict IC accessibility. Some dense urban cantons achieve short commutes; others require longer travel. Conversely, sparse rural areas show varied patterns. Geography and infrastructure design matter more than urbanization level.
